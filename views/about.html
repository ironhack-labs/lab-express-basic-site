About Marianne Moore

About Marianne

Marianne Moore was born in 1887 in St. Louis, Missouri, where she lived with her grandfather and mother. After her father passed away, Marianne moved with her mother to Carlisle, Pennsylvania, and later attended Bryn Mawr College, where she studied biology. After graduating, she continued her studies at Carlisle Commercial College and taught at Carlisle Indian School.

In 1918, she and her mother moved to New York City. She began working at the New York Public Library in 1921 and released her first volume of poetry, Poems, in the same year. She released her second collection of poetry, Observations, in 1924, and worked as the editor of the literary magazine Dial from 1925 to 1929.

Moore's influence and promise as a poet expanded in later years. She was awarded the Pulitzer Prize in 1951 for Collected Poems. She was published in Life, The New Yorker, The New York Times, and other publications. She passed away in 1972 in New York City.

About Marianne's Poetry

Although Marianne's poems advocate, at times, for restraint or modestry, they often do so with playfulness, invention, and experimentation. She famously edited one of her most famous poems, Poetry, to reduce its length from 31 lines to three, making the revision nearly two decades after first publishing the poem.
